How to Convert NIC to DOB — Sri Lankan ID Number to Birthday Decoder

  1. 1.

    Read the birth year

    The first four digits on a new-format card (or first two on an old one) encode the birth year. New: 1999 from 1999…. Old: 90 from 90….

  2. 2.

    Decode day-of-year and gender

    The next three digits are the day of the year — 1 for 1 January, 365 for 31 December. If the number exceeds 366, subtract 500 to get the actual day, and that means the cardholder is female.

  3. 3.

    Serial number and check digit

    What remains is a serial number assigned at registration. New-format cards (12 digits) end with a single check digit; old-format cards (9 digits + a letter) end with V or X.

In the old-format NIC (9 digits + letter), V means the cardholder is a registered voter, while X means they are not registered to vote. New-format 12-digit NICs replaced this letter with a numeric check digit.

Prepend “19” to the 2-digit year, keep the 3-digit day code unchanged, insert a zero before the 4-digit serial to make it 5 digits, then remove the V or X suffix. Example: 852140123V becomes 198521400123.

The 3-digit day-of-year value determines gender. Values between 1 and 366 indicate male. Values between 501 and 866 indicate female — subtract 500 to get the actual day of the year.

In the Sri Lankan NIC numbering system designed by the Department for Registration of Persons (DRP), females have 500 added to their 3-digit day of the year value (resulting in numbers from 501 to 866). This allows the system to encode both the exact date of birth and legal gender within a single 3-digit field.

New 12-digit NICs replace the old V/X voter status suffix with a numeric checksum digit at the end. It is calculated using a modified Luhn/DRP weighted modulo check algorithm across the preceding 11 digits to ensure data integrity during electronic data entry.
