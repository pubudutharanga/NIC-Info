How the DOB-to-NIC Calculation Works

1

Calculate the day of the year. Count how many days have passed since January 1 of the birth year. January 15 = day 15. June 15 = day 166 (or 167 in a leap year).

2

Add 500 for females. The DRP uses a +500 offset for female NICs. A female born on day 166 gets code 666. This lets one 3-digit field encode both birthday and gender.

3

Combine with the year. Old format: 2-digit year + day code. New format: 4-digit year + day code. The remaining digits are a unique serial assigned by DRP — we cannot generate those.

DOB to NIC — Frequently Asked Questions

No. A date of birth and gender only determine the first 5 digits (old format) or 7 digits (new format). The remaining serial digits are unique per person and assigned by the Department for Registration of Persons (DRP). No public tool can generate a full NIC from a birthday alone.

The day-of-year code is a 3-digit number encoding both birthday and gender. For males, it's the actual day number (001–366). For females, 500 is added (501–866). January 15 is day 015 for a male and 515 for a female.

Everyone born on the same date with the same gender shares the same NIC prefix. The DRP uses a 4-digit (old) or 5-digit (new) serial number to distinguish individuals born on the same day.
